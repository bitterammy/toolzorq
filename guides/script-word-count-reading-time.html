How to Check Your Script's Word Count and Reading Time Before You Record

A script that looks short on the page can run long once spoken, and a script that looks complete can turn out to be two minutes short of the video you planned. Checking the length before you record takes a minute, and it saves re-recording later. This guide shows how to do it with TOOLZORQ's free Word Counter.

What the Word Counter counts

The Word Counter is one of the free tools on TOOLZORQ's homepage. Paste an essay, a script, or an article into it, and everything is counted live as you type or paste: words, characters, characters without spaces, sentences, and paragraphs. It also shows an estimated reading time, which assumes about 200 words per minute. Everything runs in your browser — there is no sign-up and nothing to install, and your text is never sent anywhere.

To use it:

  1. Open TOOLZORQ and find the Word Counter among the tools.
  2. Paste your script into the box. The counts update live — you do not need to press anything.
  3. Read the word count and the estimated reading time, and compare them with the length you are aiming for.
  4. Edit in your document, paste again, and watch the numbers move. Trimming and re-pasting is the whole workflow.

Reading time is an estimate, not a recording time

The reading-time figure assumes silent reading at about 200 words per minute. Speaking a script aloud is usually slower: you pause between thoughts, emphasise words, and leave room for the listener to follow. So treat the reading time as a floor, not a promise — your spoken version will most likely run longer than the figure shown. If the estimate already exceeds your target length, the script is definitely too long. If it sits just under your target, you are probably about right, with the pauses and emphasis filling the gap.

The one reliable way to know your own pace is to time yourself once: read a script of known length aloud, note how long it took, and use that as your personal yardstick for the next one. After that, the Word Counter's figures become much more useful, because you know how they translate into your voice.

What each count is actually good for

Words are the best single measure of a script's length. If you write regularly, you will quickly learn roughly how many words fill the video, lesson, or talk you usually make, and that number becomes your planning target.

Characters matter where a platform sets a limit — a video description, a post, or a form field. Pasting the text into the counter before you post it tells you whether it fits, instead of finding out from an error message after you have written it.

Characters without spaces are useful when a limit or a price is quoted that way, which some platforms and services do. Having both figures side by side means you do not have to guess which one applies.

Sentences and paragraphs reveal structure at a glance. A script with very few, very long sentences is hard to speak naturally; breaking it into shorter sentences and clear paragraphs usually makes it easier to deliver and easier for a listener to follow. If the paragraph count is low but the word count is high, that is a sign to break the text up.

Rule of thumb: check the length before you record, not after. A two-minute trim on paper is a small edit; a two-minute cut from a finished recording is a re-record.

Quick checklist

  • Is the script pasted into the Word Counter, with the counts updating live?
  • Does the word count match the length you are aiming for?
  • Is the estimated reading time under your target — leaving room for pauses and emphasis?
  • Are the sentences short enough to speak naturally, in clear paragraphs?
  • Have you re-checked the counts after your final edit?
