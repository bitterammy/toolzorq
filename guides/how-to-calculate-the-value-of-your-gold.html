How to Calculate the Value of Your Gold: Karat, Weight, Tola and the Melt-Value Formula

Short answer: the value of your gold is its weight in grams, multiplied by its purity (karat divided by 24), multiplied by the price of one gram of pure gold. So 1 tola of 24-karat gold is worth exactly the current per-tola rate, while 1 tola of 22-karat gold is worth 22/24 of that rate — about 91.7 per cent.

Before you sell a gold bangle, a chain, or an old set you inherited, work out its value yourself. Gold is bought and sold by weight and purity, and the maths is simple once you know the formula. This guide explains the melt-value formula, the karat scale, the tola, masha and ratti units, and TOOLZORQ's free Gold Value Calculator.

What is melt value?

Melt value is the value of the pure gold inside a piece, melted down and sold as raw gold. It is the floor price of your gold: the value of the metal itself, before making charges and before any buyer's margin. A shop sells jewellery for more than melt value, and buys old gold for less.

That is why melt value is the number to calculate first: an offer far below it is a bad offer, and an offer at or near it is a fair one. It depends on three things — weight, purity, and today's price of pure gold — taken from the scale, the karat stamp, and the sarafa rate.

Karat and purity: how much of your gold is actually gold?

Pure gold is 24 karat, but too soft to wear, so jewellery gold is mixed with other metals — usually silver, copper or zinc — to make it harder. The karat number is the fraction of the piece that is gold: divide the karat by 24. So 22-karat gold is about 91.7 per cent pure, and 18-karat gold is 75 per cent pure.

Jewellers also stamp gold with a three-digit hallmark number: 916 means 22 karat, and 750 means 18 karat.

In Pakistan, the commonest jewellery gold is 22 karat; 21 karat is also common, and 18 karat is used for lighter pieces. 24-karat gold is almost never made into jewellery — it bends too easily — and is sold as bars and coins. The karat matters: 1 tola of 22K is worth about 8.3 per cent less than 1 tola of 24K at the same rate, and 1 tola of 18K is worth a quarter less. Mistaking 18K for 22K overvalues your gold by about 22 per cent, so check the stamp.

Check the karat before the price. A 10-gram piece of 22K gold holds 9.17 grams of pure gold; the same 10 grams in 18K holds only 7.5 grams.

Weight units: tola, masha, ratti, grams and troy ounces

Gold in Pakistan is weighed in tola, divided into masha and ratti — the traditional units also used for land (see the marla guide). One tola is 11.6638 grams: 12 masha, each of 8 ratti, so 96 ratti to the tola. "2 tola 3 masha" is 2 + 3/12 = 2.25 tola, about 26.24 grams.

Outside South Asia, gold is priced per troy ounce: 31.1035 grams, heavier than the ordinary ounce of 28.35 grams. International prices are quoted per troy ounce, so a per-gram price means dividing by 31.1035, not 28.35. Mixing them up overstates the value by nearly 10 per cent.

The melt-value formula, step by step

Step 1 — find the price of one gram of pure gold. Divide the sarafa rate per tola by 11.6638, or an international spot price per troy ounce by 31.1035.

Step 2 — work out how much pure gold your piece contains. Multiply the weight in grams by the purity (karat divided by 24). A 10-gram piece of 22K gold contains 10 × 22/24 = 9.17 grams of pure gold.

Step 3 — multiply the pure gold by the price per gram. That gives the melt value: value = weight in grams × (karat ÷ 24) × price of one gram of pure gold.

A useful shortcut when the rate is quoted per tola, as it is in Pakistan: value = weight in tola × (karat ÷ 24) × rate per tola. The grams cancel out, so no conversion is needed. For example, 2 tola of 22K gold at a rate of 300,000 PKR per tola is worth 2 × 22/24 × 300,000 = 550,000 PKR.

Worked examples with the arithmetic shown

All examples below use one example rate — 300,000 PKR per tola — the example rate on the TOOLZORQ Gold Value Calculator page. It is an example only.

Example 1 — 1 tola of 24-karat gold. 1 tola of 24K at the example rate is worth exactly 1 × 24/24 × 300,000 = 300,000 PKR — the price of one tola of pure gold.

Example 2 — 1 gram of 24-karat gold. The price of one gram of pure gold is 300,000 ÷ 11.6638 = about 25,721 PKR per gram — the building block for the rest.

Example 3 — 2 tola 3 masha of 22-karat gold. First convert the weight: 2 tola 3 masha is 2 + 3/12 = 2.25 tola. Then 2.25 × 22/24 × 300,000: 2.25 × 300,000 = 675,000, and 675,000 × 22/24 = 618,750. So the piece is worth about 618,750 PKR at the example rate. In 24K it would be worth 675,000 PKR — the 22K purity costs 56,250 PKR.

Example 4 — 10 grams of 21-karat gold. The pure gold is 10 × 21/24 = 8.75 grams. At the example rate, one gram of pure gold is about 25,721 PKR, so the value is 8.75 × 25,721 = about 225,059 PKR. In tola: 10 grams is 0.857 tola, and 0.857 × 21/24 × 300,000 = about 225,059 PKR.

Example 5 — 5 tola of 18-karat gold. A heavier piece in a lower karat: 5 × 18/24 × 300,000. 5 × 300,000 = 1,500,000, and 1,500,000 × 18/24 = 1,125,000. So 5 tola of 18K gold is worth 1,125,000 PKR at the example rate — less than 2 tola of 24K gold, though it weighs more than twice as much. Weight alone does not decide value; karat decides it too.

Example 6 — a US-dollar example. Suppose, as a clearly labelled example, the spot price is $3,700 per troy ounce. One gram of pure gold is 3,700 ÷ 31.1035 = about $118.96. A 10-gram chain in 22K gold contains 10 × 22/24 = 9.17 grams of pure gold, so its melt value is 9.17 × 118.96 = about $1,090.

Melt value is not the shop price

Melt value is not what a shop charges and not what a shop pays. A new piece costs the melt value plus making charges, and often plus wastage: making charges are the goldsmith's labour, design and finishing, while wastage is a charge for gold supposedly lost as filings and dust during making. Question wastage: the gold actually lost is tiny beside the percentages some shops quote.

When a jeweller buys your old gold, the price is the melt value minus the buyer's margin: the buyer must test, melt, refine and resell the gold, and each step costs money, so no dealer pays the full melt value. A fair buy-back offer is usually a few per cent below melt value; an offer 15 or 20 per cent below it is a bad one. Get quotes from more than one buyer, and compare each with the melt value.

Two things do not count in the melt value. First, stones and gems: a diamond or ruby set into a ring is not gold, and its weight must be subtracted. If a ring weighs 8 grams but the stone weighs 1 gram, only 7 grams are gold. Second, the design: an antique can be worth more than melt value to a collector, but to a dealer buying by weight it is worth melt value and no more.

How to weigh and check your gold

Weigh the piece on a proper digital scale that reads to at least 0.01 grams — a kitchen scale is not accurate enough for small pieces, and a bathroom scale is useless. Many jewellers will weigh a piece for free, but watch the scale yourself; weigh it at home first.

Check the hallmark: a small stamp inside a ring band, on a chain clasp, or on a bangle. It will read 916, 875, 750, 585 or 417, or say 22K, 21K, 18K, 14K or 10K; the table above converts the stamp to a karat. If there is no stamp, have the piece tested before you agree a price — a jeweller can test gold with an acid test or an electronic tester in a few minutes.

Remove stones from the weight: ask the jeweller to weigh the gold separately, or to tell you the stone weight so you can subtract it. Keep the original receipt — it states the weight and karat — and keep any new receipt too, with weight, karat, rate, making charges and wastage on it.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using the wrong ounce. International prices are per troy ounce (31.1035 g), not per ordinary ounce (28.35 g). Dividing by 28.35 overstates the per-gram price by nearly 10 per cent.
  • Forgetting the karat. Valuing 18K gold as if it were 22K overstates the value by about 22 per cent. Check the stamp or get the piece tested.
  • Counting stones as gold. Stones are not gold: subtract the stone weight first.
  • Using an old rate. Gold rates move every day; last month's rate can be thousands of rupees per tola out of date.
  • Confusing melt value with the selling price. A new piece costs melt value plus making charges and wastage; an old piece sells for melt value minus the buyer's margin.
  • Trusting one buyer's quote. Get quotes from more than one buyer and compare each with the melt value. The first offer is rarely the best.
  • Mixing up tola and grams. One tola is 11.6638 grams, not 10 or 12 grams. Rounding it to 10 grams understates the value by about 14 per cent.

How do I calculate the value of my gold?

How much is 1 tola of gold worth?

How many grams are in one tola of gold?

What is the difference between 22K and 24K gold?

What is the melt value of gold?

It is the floor price of the metal: shops sell new jewellery for more, because of making charges, and buy old gold for less, because of their own costs.
